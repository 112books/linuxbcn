Moving from Blogger to your own site

Three thousand posts are not a problem if it is done in order

18/11/2026

What to consider when leaving Blogger: images hosted by Google, addresses, comments and tags, with the real 9 Barris Imatge case.

The short answer

Leaving Blogger is mostly about taking the content out of Google’s cloud: posts, images and comments. Addresses are kept, but images must be downloaded and hosted on your own infrastructure, because today they live on Google servers.

Why people leave

  • Control: content sits on a platform that decides for you (design, templates, features).
  • Speed and looks: Blogger templates are far behind what can be done today.
  • Trust: platforms that shut services or change terms. It has happened before.
  • Sovereignty: photos, texts and comments are yours, but you do not hold them.

What to preserve

  • The posts, with date, author and tags.
  • The images, which today point at Google servers.
  • The addresses of each post and each tag.
  • The comments, if you want to keep them.
  • The domain, which is what makes nothing change place.

The 9 Barris Imatge case

A photography collective in Nou Barris has documented the neighbourhood since 2002, and since 2008 published it on Blogger. 3,006 posts written by people who do photography, not IT.

The migration moved every post, image, album and tag with zero errors, keeping the addresses. The new system generates more than 8,700 pages in under half a minute. All the detail is in the project page.

What changes for the people publishing

Before, publishing meant logging into Blogger and writing. After, it means opening a simple manager, writing and saving. The difference: the site no longer depends on an external platform and the photos are at home.

Frequently asked questions

Will images be lost when leaving Blogger?

If they are not downloaded on purpose, yes: Blogger images have always been served from Google servers and may stop being served. Downloading them and hosting them with the project is part of the job.

What about comments?

They are kept as static content inside each post, with author and date. If you want new comments, an external service can be connected or they can be left closed.

Do I need to keep the custom domain?

Yes, and it is what makes keeping addresses possible. If the blog is on a blogspot subdomain, the previous step is owning a domain and configuring it properly first.

How long does the old blog stay online?

Whatever it takes to check everything. Nothing is disconnected until the new site is stable and the addresses respond.
