Hugo or WordPress: which one fits you

The honest comparison, without selling you mine

18/11/2026

Hugo and WordPress compared for cultural, artistic and small business sites: maintenance, speed, editing and the real cost of each option.

The short answer

Hugo if the site publishes information and you want few parts that can break. WordPress if you need a shop, users, a complex editorial workflow or an ecosystem of extensions that already covers your case.

The comparison, in one table

CriterionHugo (static site)WordPress
MaintenanceAlmost none: no databaseConstant: core, plugins and backups
SpeedVery high: pre-built filesDepends on hosting and plugins
Attack surfaceSmall: no admin panelLarge: the most attacked target on the web
EditingVisual editor on text filesPanel with menus and roles
Shop and usersNo (needs external services)Yes, built in
ExtensionsFew, and often not neededThousands, with their maintenance
Bulk publishingVery goodVery good
People who can work on itFewer, but you never need toMany, and often you do

When Hugo fits you

  • Sites from 5 to 200 pages that inform about something: a cultural space, a band, a studio, a publisher, a restaurant.
  • Projects where nobody wants to think about updates.
  • Sites that must load fast on mobile and work in difficult conditions.
  • Projects where visitors’ data must not pass through third parties.

When WordPress fits you

  • A shop with stock, variants, coupons and shipping (WooCommerce).
  • A member area or courses with registrations and student tracking.
  • An editorial workflow with many roles, calendars and approvals.
  • When your team is already trained in WordPress and you just want to maintain it well.
  • When you depend on a very specific plugin that does exactly what you need.

What an agency selling WordPress will not tell you

That the real cost is not building the site: it is maintaining it. A site with fifteen plugins is monthly work someone must do — or nobody does. Many security and speed problems we see are not WordPress, they are that work not being paid for.

What an agency selling static sites will not tell you

That not everything can be done. If you need a complex shop or a member area, a static site does not reach and bolting on external services can end up costing more and depending on third parties.

Frequently asked questions

Is Hugo hard to learn?

For editing content, no: the editor is visual and looks like a form. For custom coding, yes: templates are involved, and that is a professional’s job. The client never touches code.

Is WordPress not easier because everyone knows it?

It is easier to find someone who knows it, and that is a real argument. It is also easier to find someone who left it half done or full of plugins. Ease of finding hands is not the same as ease of maintaining it well.

What if I want to publish every day?

Both can do it. With WordPress you get more extensions for unusual things; with Hugo you get a simpler editor and a site that does not need updating. If you publish a lot with many people, weigh it carefully.

Which is cheaper?

It depends how you count. Building it is not the same as maintaining it for seven years. A static site usually needs less maintenance; WordPress usually needs more care hours. We do not quote without knowing the case.
