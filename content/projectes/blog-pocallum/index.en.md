## Goodbye WordPress, hello Hugo

The Blog de Pocallum —a photographic chronicle of concerts, festivals, cameras and neighbourhood life in Barcelona since 2010— has switched engines entirely, and it's not the first time. The result of this latest step: more speed, less attack surface, and the exact same content at the exact same URLs.

---

## Three eras, one blog

The blog hasn't always lived on WordPress. It started on **Blogger**, Google's free platform: convenient to get going, but with content, data and design under a third party's control, with neither sovereignty nor long-term predictability.

The move to **WordPress** was already a step towards free software: open source, control over your own content, no longer depending on a single company to keep existing. It served well for 15 years, but over time it accumulated the weight typical of any database-backed CMS: plugins, constant updates, and an attack surface that kept growing.

With **Hugo**, the cycle closes with the final step: a static site, no database, no third-party dependencies, hosted on our own servers. Full data sovereignty, no intermediary, nothing left to exploit. From Google's cloud to free software, and from free software to full sovereignty: each step has made the blog a little cleaner, simpler, purer and more proactive.

---

## Why leave WordPress

WordPress served well for a decade, but it had accumulated problems that couldn't be ignored:

- **Weight and slowness:** 2,353 posts, 137,000 words, 96 categories and 3,177 tags, served by a CMS running on MySQL and PHP.
- **Security:** WordPress is a massive target for bots and exploits; every plugin is one more attack surface.
- **Maintenance:** constant updates to core, plugins and themes, and patches with every release.
- **Historical vulnerability:** with 15 years of history, any forgotten version was a risk.

---

## The migration process

The migration was done step by step, always with the safety net of being able to roll back:

1. **Full export** of WordPress via XML —all the content: posts, categories, tags and metadata.
2. **Conversion to Markdown** with the `wordpress-export-to-markdown` tool (v3.0.5): 2,353 posts (plus 10 drafts) converted into clean `.md` files.
3. **Post-processing** with a custom script that recovered thumbnails, featured images and tags from the canonical source, and resolved escaped Vimeo shortcodes.
4. **A dedicated Hugo theme**, inheriting the parent site's design system but adapted for the blog: chronological listing, monthly archive, mosaic layout, lightbox and static search.
5. **Full URL parity:** every WordPress permalink (`/YYYY/MM/DD/slug/`) stays identical. No existing link —external, search results, RSS— breaks. Verified with a 1:1 crawler (2,360 URLs checked).
6. **SEO:** Yoast titles and meta descriptions preserved, JSON-LD, Open Graph and Twitter Cards generated by the theme, no plugins involved.

---

## Advantages

- **Performance:** no PHP, no MySQL. Pure, minified HTML, served fast even from basic hosting.
- **Security:** nothing left to exploit —no admin panel, no database, no plugins.
- **Full control:** content lives in Git, versioned, clonable, with no dependency on a closed CMS. Edited in Markdown.
- **Static search (Pagefind):** full-text search, indexed at build time, no server or cookies.
- **GDPR-friendly analytics:** GoatCounter, cookie-free, using the same counter already in place.
- **Simple management:** text and images in files, nothing in a database.
- **Frictionless editing:** **Sveltia CMS**, adapted to our own taste and needs —visual content editing with no need to touch Markdown by hand.

## The result, in numbers

- **2,353 posts and 137,000 words** of content, migrated in full.
- **71 legacy comments** preserved as static content within the posts.
- One single Markdown file per post, with complete frontmatter (date, categories, tags, thumbnails).

## Why Hugo

Hugo is a static site generator written in Go: it compiles 2,300+ pages in seconds, with no runtime dependency and a clean, powerful templating system. For a photography blog with 15 years of history, Hugo does exactly what it needs to: turn Markdown into fast HTML and keep the content within reach of the person who made it.

The whole migration —with its planned cutover: full backup, HTML deployment, URL-by-URL verification, and only then decommissioning WordPress— is a process anyone running a WordPress blog can replicate. The blog is now faster, safer, and 15 years of photographs are still exactly where they belong.
